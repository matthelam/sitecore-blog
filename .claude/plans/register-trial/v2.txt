We've started to settle on how we hand context to an agent. Instruction files at the repository root, skills, layered system prompts, a handful of memory approaches. It's a real maturing of the craft, and it's good at one thing: documenting what I'll call **Prescriptive Context** - the rules and standing guidance an agent is told to follow.
===
Prescription only goes so far. In practice I keep stepping back into the loop - to ask the right question, to nudge the agent down a different path, to supply a hard-won insight or point it at the right tool at the right moment. That accumulated human experience is a different kind of context: less structured than the rules, more the _ah-ha_ moment that saves the day. I call it **Reflective Context**, and right now it's neither here nor there. The memory containers are very sophisticated, but I reckon there's a heap more clarity to surface in how we hook them up.
===
I'll admit it: I originally started compiling all my research and testing into one massive white paper. I kept finding issues in my logic or my testing, and kept finding real implementations worth looking into. I've been stuck for two months just trying to articulate what I've prototyped, and I'm missing out on the latest updates in the field. So this is me getting the framework down, with progressive posts as updates. I'll also write up each major platform, in a more structured way, and how it lines up with the Legal Framework. To be clear: there are a LOT of tools out there now, all edging closer to my idea - some may even argue it's surpassed my framework. That's fine. I'll keep documenting the real implementation across different platforms, and hopefully it serves as a useful baseline for all of us.
===
The ecosystem has stabilised on a well-described structure for _prescriptive_ rules. We also worked out early that an agent needs memory - or something closer to fine-tuning through context. Those are the RAG and vector stores, the LLM wiki, the MCP data sources.
===
I'm not challenging that we have those things. I'm challenging **the way we reach for them, and the moment we do it**. We load memory the way we load everything else - up front, indiscriminately, as part of the standing context. That's the wrong time and the wrong shape for experience. **Experience is precedent. It only matters once the rules in front of you have run out.**
