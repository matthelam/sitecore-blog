## The Same Pre-Pattern Moment

I was deep into the third restructure of the [skills engine](/blog/sub-agents-vs-personas-vs-skills-a-journey-through-code-architecture) when it clicked: the agent space is in the same moment the web was before MVC. One developer's controller was another's helper, and another's just-a-file-I-put-code-in. Then came strongly typed roles and eventual enforcement, and the ambiguity collapsed.

That rush gave us patterns like SOLID and MVC, and today they're everywhere. I think we're in the middle of the next one.

## AI Is a New Medium, Not a New Epistemology

The problems agents solve (coordination, decomposition, knowledge management, quality gates) existed before LLMs. Accumulated software wisdom still applies. The question is whether we recognise it, or reinvent it poorly.

What's new is that we're naming the parts before we've agreed on the shape. Terms like **skills** and **harness** have appeared in the last year, and everyone, me included, is madly rushing to figure out how they fit together.

## Mind, Body and Soul

I'm almost thinking the pattern that's emerging hints at something like Mind, Body and Soul:

- **Soul:** the Constitution. The values and supreme laws the agent holds to, whatever it's working on.
- **Mind:** the Legislation and the Memory. The rules for the work in front of it, and the experience it has built up along the way.
- **Body:** the tools. What it actually acts with.

Looking back over my own work, I keep landing on these same three seams under different names:

| Where it shows up                                                      | Soul                       | Mind                               | Body                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| [Legal Framework](/blog/a-legal-framework-for-agent-context)           | Constitution               | Legislation and Common Law         | the tools it governs                       |
| [Cognitive profiles](/blog/composing-agent-teams-by-cognitive-profile) | how the agent thinks       | skills: what it does and knows     | the harness: how it gets done              |
| My harness tiers                                                       | cognitive profile (tier 3) | agent guidance and skills (tier 2) | tool config: linters, CI, scripts (tier 1) |

That's my rough mapping, not a settled one, and the edges are blurry. Skills feel like Mind, but they carry capability too. An agent's way of thinking could be Soul, or it could be Mind. This is a thought, not an answer. I'm thinking aloud about where I'm at today, and like everyone else I'm madly rushing to formulate my own string theory.

## It Pointed to the Legal Framework

When I first wrote this post, it pointed towards what became my [Legal Framework for agent context](/blog/a-legal-framework-for-agent-context). The framework is different now, but it held a similar ideal: separation of concerns along these seams. The Constitution is the Soul. The Legislation and the Common Law, its memory, are the Mind. The tools are the Body. I wrote up how that runs day to day in [Implementing the Legal Framework](/blog/implementing-the-legal-framework).

I'm happy to say I had to come back and rewrite this post. My first version mapped SOLID's five principles onto agents, and I felt there was more value in where my thinking has gone since.

## A Draft Thesis: Come Argue With Me

This is still a draft thesis, not a settled pattern.

The enforcement that made SOLID actionable was linters, type systems and code review norms. Whatever settles the agent space will need its own enforcement, and hopefully through continued discussion we can establish it, the same way SOLID did.

If you've built multi-agent systems and think I'm wrong, I want to hear it. That's how patterns settle.
